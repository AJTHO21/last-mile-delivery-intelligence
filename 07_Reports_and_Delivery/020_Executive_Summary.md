# Executive summary | Last-mile dispatch exceptions

**Final draft for review · Simulated engagement · 20 November 2026**

Prioritize severe late arrivals within comparable countries and route sizes. Use resequencing and anomaly flags to guide the review.

- **11.13% of eligible deliveries arrived late** (23,718 of 213,122). Among late arrivals, the median delay was **47.94 minutes** and P90 was **206.09 minutes** past the recorded window close. **43.27% of eligible routes** had at least one late arrival (8,387 of 19,383).
- **The pooled late-route gap shrank after standardization.** Changed-address routes had a crude rate of 45.74%, compared with 36.37% for unchanged order. Weighting both groups to the same country and route-size distribution gave 42.27% and 42.42%, respectively, across six supported strata. The changed-minus-unchanged gap was −0.15 percentage points. It was +0.02 after excluding routes with timing spans over 24 hours.
- **The median paired distance change was 0.00 geodesic km.** There were 7,386 shorter, 5,291 equal and 6,970 longer routes. Distance checks flagged 98 routes. Straight-line Haversine legs describe the recorded geometry. Mileage and costs require road or vehicle data.

**Recommended first step:** Pilot a review of high-lateness deliveries with timing flags visible. Ask the data owner to validate long spans and reversals, and capture service-start times and exception reasons before evaluating operational changes. These findings describe one operator's records in Norway and Denmark. They establish no causal effect of resequencing.
